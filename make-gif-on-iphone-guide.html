Make a GIF on iPhone from video in 5 steps.

The short answer

Choose the output before choosing the method.

If you only want a Live Photo to move repeatedly inside Apple-compatible apps, Photos may be enough. If the destination expects a .gif file, or you need editing controls, export a real GIF.

Apple's Live Photos guide explains the built-in Loop and Bounce effects.

Loop

Repeats a Live Photo continuously. It is fast, but it does not provide frame-by-frame editing.

Bounce

Plays the motion forward and backward. It can hide a harsh transition without creating a custom sequence.

Real GIF export

Use this for a portable GIF file, custom frame timing, captions, canvas ratios, reverse playback, or a refined boomerang loop.

Five-step workflow

How to turn a video into a GIF on iPhone.

Start with the shortest useful source. Every extra second creates more frames, increases the file size, and makes a clean loop harder to build.

1. Choose a video or Live Photo

Open GIFmaker and choose the short video or Live Photo that contains the action you want to loop.

2. Keep the useful frames

Review the generated frames and remove unneeded frames so the loop starts and ends close to the action.

3. Tune timing and playback

Adjust frame delays, reorder the filmstrip, and choose normal, reverse, or boomerang playback while watching the live preview.

4. Set the canvas and caption

Choose an aspect ratio and fit, then add a short text overlay only if the animation needs context.

5. Preview and export

Play the complete loop, confirm the crop and pacing, then save or share the exported GIF.

Make a real GIF

Quality and file size

Keep the loop clear, short, and intentional.

A good GIF is not simply the highest-resolution export. It balances readable detail with a file size the destination can load and play smoothly.

Trim before styling

Remove the idle beginning and ending first. A shorter source gives every later decision a cleaner foundation.

Use fewer purposeful frames

Remove repeated frames that do not change the action. Keep enough frames for smooth motion, but do not preserve every moment by default.

Match the canvas to the destination

Use square for general sharing, portrait for vertical posts, or landscape when the action depends on width.

Preview the first-to-last transition

The most visible flaw is often the jump where the GIF restarts. Reverse or boomerang playback can help when the source has no natural return.

Common questions

Making GIFs on iPhone.

How do I turn a Live Photo into a GIF on iPhone?

Open the Live Photo in GIFmaker, keep the useful frames, adjust timing and playback, preview the loop, then export a real GIF. Apple's Photos app can apply Loop or Bounce, but those effects do not provide the same frame and export controls.

How do I turn an iPhone video into a GIF?

Import a short video into GIFmaker, keep the frames around the useful action, adjust timing and playback, preview the loop, then export it as a GIF.

Why is my iPhone GIF file so large?

GIF size grows with pixel dimensions, frame count, duration, and visual detail. Shorten the clip, remove redundant frames, use a smaller canvas, and avoid long high-motion scenes.

Does GIFmaker upload my video or Live Photo?

The current App Store listing says the core editing workflow runs on device, requires no account, and does not upload source media. Apple's privacy label displays Data Not Collected.

Should I use normal, reverse, or boomerang playback?

Use normal playback for actions with a natural restart, reverse for a deliberate backward effect, and boomerang when returning to the first frame makes the loop feel smoother.
