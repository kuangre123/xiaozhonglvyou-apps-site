Deep dive

Why classification-first cleanup gives better results.

Most photo cleaner apps start by asking you to delete things. AI Cleaning starts by helping you understand what is in your library. This matters because a receipt, a group photo, and a screenshot need different cleanup decisions. Batch-deleting without classification risks losing photos you actually need.

Google Photos groups photos by people and places, which is useful for browsing but not for cleanup decisions. AI Cleaning groups by practical categories — documents you might need, food photos you can likely delete, duplicate sets where you keep the best one.

Document categories matter

Receipts, invoices, and ID cards should never be in a "delete these" batch. AI Cleaning flags them separately so you can archive what matters.

Storage numbers are real

AI Cleaning shows actual device storage per category — not cloud quota estimates. You see exactly how much space screenshots, duplicates, and large videos consume.

Review-first means no regrets

Every cleanup group is a review queue, not a one-tap delete. You decide what stays and what goes, photo by photo or batch by batch.

Privacy comparison

On-device AI vs cloud-based photo analysis.

Privacy is the biggest differentiator. AI Cleaning uses Apple's Core ML framework to run all photo classification directly on your iPhone. Your photos never leave your device. Google Photos and Gemini Photos require uploading photos to Google's cloud servers for their AI features to work — which means your personal photos, documents, and ID cards pass through external servers.

AI Cleaning: on-device

Core ML processing. No server calls. No photo uploads. Works offline.

Google Photos: cloud-based

AI features require Google account and cloud sync. Photos leave your device.

Why it matters

If your library contains sensitive documents, receipts with financial data, or ID cards, on-device processing eliminates the risk of cloud exposure.
