Menu translation first

How to translate a restaurant menu on iPhone.

Start with Apple's built-in Translate app. Choose the printed-menu or saved-photo route below; a separate travel app is optional.

Supported languages and features vary by iOS version and region. Test the language pair you need before traveling.

Printed menu: use the live camera

  1. Open Translate and tap Camera.
  2. Choose the languages and point the camera at the printed menu. Translated text appears over the live view.
  3. Tap Take Picture to freeze the view. Tap overlaid text to copy it, save a favorite, or play it aloud.

See Apple's camera translation steps.

Saved menu photo: choose it from your library

  1. Open Translate, tap Camera, then tap Photo Library.
  2. Select the menu photo and review the translation against the original.

Apple documents this route in its Photo Library instructions.

Already in Photos? Open the image, tap Live Text, select text, then tap Translate. If needed, enable Live Text in Settings, General, Language & Region. Live Text requires iPhone XS/XR or later with iOS 15 or later; translation availability varies by language and region.

See Apple's Live Text requirements and translation steps.

Check the result before ordering

Compare dish names and prices with the original. Ask staff to confirm ingredients and allergens; a machine translation alone is not enough to establish that a dish is safe for you.

When an optional travel app helps

For follow-up voice questions, spoken replies, or longer conversations, review Translation Specialist features and requirements, or compare Apple Translate, Google Translate, and Translation Specialist.

Translation Specialist requires iOS 17.4 or later and is free to download with optional in-app purchases. Its offline support covers core language pairs only; test your exact pair and mode before relying on it without a connection.

Step by step

How to translate menus, signs, and conversations while traveling.

Match the mode to the moment, then test the exact language pair before relying on it away from a network.

1. Choose and test the language pair

Select the source and target languages for the destination. Translate one short phrase before leaving reliable Wi-Fi so you know the pair is configured correctly.

2. Use two-way voice conversation

For directions, hotel check-in, or a quick question, tap to talk and let the other person answer in the selected language pair.

3. Play translated speech

Let the app read the translated reply aloud when passing the phone or showing text would interrupt the exchange.

4. Scan menus, signs, and labels

For a restaurant menu, start with the iPhone Translate camera flow or open camera translation in your travel app. Take a clear photo or choose an image, then review the recognized and translated text. Keep the text level, well lit, and close enough to read.

5. Use live interpretation for longer talks

Switch to continuous interpretation for a tour, meeting, or longer exchange where repeated tap-to-talk turns are inconvenient.

6. Confirm offline support before departure

The App Store listing limits offline support to core language pairs. Test the exact pair and mode you expect to use without a network connection before the trip.

Travel FAQ

Common travel translation questions.

Can I translate a restaurant menu on iPhone without installing another app?

Yes. Start with Apple's Translate app camera mode or Live Text in Camera or Photos when the language is supported. Use a dedicated travel translator when you also need two-way voice, audio playback, live interpretation, or offline trip testing.

When should I use camera translation instead of voice?

Use camera OCR when the source is written text: menus, signs, labels, receipts, and short documents.

When is voice translation better?

Use voice input for live conversation, quick questions, and situations where typing would interrupt the exchange.

Which portfolio app is built for this?

Translation Specialist is the travel-focused iPhone app for two-way voice, translated playback, camera OCR, live interpretation, text translation, and offline support for core language pairs.

Can camera translation read a whole page reliably?

Camera OCR works best with clear, well-lit, short text. For dense documents or critical instructions, scan smaller sections and verify names, numbers, dates, and safety details.

Should medical, legal, or immigration translations be checked by a person?

Yes. Use machine translation for everyday travel assistance, but confirm critical medical, legal, safety, or immigration information with a qualified human interpreter.

Capture and verify

Capture readable text, then verify OCR and translation.

Improve the capture first

Keep the menu flat and the camera nearly parallel to its text. Avoid glare and shadows. Photograph long menus in sections while keeping dish names, ingredients and prices together. Enlarge the original and check individual characters; compression cannot restore blurred writing.

Resize a copy only when transfer requires it

If a large file needs a smaller sharing copy, try the image tool. Crop irrelevant surroundings in your photo editor first; this browser tool changes size and format rather than cropping. Compare small letters and punctuation after export. Keep the original when the reduced copy loses readable detail.

Check recognition separately from translation

Compare OCR line order, numbers and dish names against the photograph before reading the translation. Ask staff about uncertain ingredients or dietary requirements. The web image tool does not perform OCR or translation; choose the camera, saved-photo or typed-text route described above in an app that supports it.
